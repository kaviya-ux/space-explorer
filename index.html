01 — The Neighborhood

Eight Worlds, In Order

Each planet drawn in pure CSS, sized and colored to give a feel for what's actually out there — from the scorched rock closest to the sun to the icy giant at the edge of the map.

Mercury

The fastest planet, and the closest — a cratered rock with almost no atmosphere to speak of.

88 day orbit · No moons

Venus

Wrapped in thick, reflective clouds, and hot enough to melt lead at the surface.

225 day orbit · No moons

Earth

The only known planet with liquid oceans, breathable air, and something to call it home.

365 day orbit · 1 moon

Mars

Rust-red and dry today, but its riverbeds hint at a much wetter past.

687 day orbit · 2 moons

Jupiter

The solar system's largest planet, banded in storms — including one older than any human record.

12 year orbit · 95 moons

Saturn

Famous for its rings — countless pieces of ice and rock caught in a perfect, thin halo.

29 year orbit · 146 moons

Uranus

An ice giant that spins on its side, giving it the strangest seasons of any planet.

84 year orbit · 28 moons

Neptune

The farthest planet from the sun, with the fastest winds ever recorded in the solar system.

165 year orbit · 16 moons

02 — The Journey So Far

Missions That Left Earth

A short timeline of the launches that turned the solar system from a diagram into a place we've actually visited.

  1. 1957

    Sputnik 1

    The first artificial satellite reaches orbit, and the space age formally begins.

  2. 1969

    Apollo 11

    Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin become the first humans to walk on the Moon.

  3. 1977

    Voyager 1 & 2

    Twin probes launch to tour the outer planets, and are still transmitting from interstellar space.

  4. 1997

    Mars Pathfinder

    The first rover, Sojourner, rolls across the Martian surface and sends back close-up images.

  5. 2015

    New Horizons

    After a nine-year journey, the probe returns the first clear photographs of Pluto.

  6. 2021

    James Webb Space Telescope

    The largest telescope ever launched begins capturing the deepest images of the universe yet taken.
